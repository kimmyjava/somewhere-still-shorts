**P1 — Hook** (shot 1–2, ~0.0–9.3s)
> Ever gotten a message that just says, can we talk tomorrow? And suddenly
> your brain starts preparing for a disaster that hasn't even happened yet?

**P2 — The alarm system** (shot 3–4, ~9.3–21.1s)
> Here's the thing. Anxiety is your brain's alarm system. Its whole job is to
> protect you. Sometimes it just treats an ordinary moment like a five alarm
> emergency.

**P3 — Reframe** (shot 4 tail, ~21.1–25.1s)
> Your brain isn't your enemy. It's just being a little too protective.

**P4 — Four faces of anxiety** (shot 5, ~25.1–38.9s)
> So what does that look like? Replaying a conversation from three years ago.
> A racing heart and sweaty hands. Avoiding that one email. Or a tiny problem
> growing into a giant monster in your imagination.

**P5 — Interactive pause** (shot 6, ~38.9–45.3s)
> Which one sounds most familiar? Take a second. Really.

**P6 — Technique 1: name it** (shot 7, ~45.3–52.9s)
> And when it shows up, you've got options. First, name it. I'm feeling
> anxious right now. A feeling is not automatically a fact.

**P7 — Technique 2: ground yourself** (shot 8, ~53.0–64.2s)
> Next, ground yourself. Name five things you can see. Four you can touch.
> Three you can hear. It pulls you right back into the present.

**P8 — Technique 3: slow down** (shot 9, ~64.2–69.3s)
> And finally, slow down. One gentle breath. You don't have to solve every
> problem in your head today.

**P9 — Callback / takeaway** (shot 10, ~69.3–76.1s)
> Remember, your brain is trying to protect you. You just don't have to
> believe every scary story it tells you.

**P10 — Disclaimer + interactive ending** (shots 11–12, ~76.2–89.5s)
> And if anxiety starts running your everyday life, talking to a professional
> is a strong move, not a weakness. Now tell me, what's your brain's favorite
> way of overthinking? Drop your answer in the comments.

## Educational-accuracy notes
- No diagnosis or disorder claims; anxiety framed as a normal alarm system.
- Techniques are optional coping moves ("you've got options"), not cures.
- Professional help is framed as strength (P10) without minimizing severe
  anxiety.
- Gentle humor lands on the situation (phone panic, "literally ALL of them"),
  never on anxious people.
